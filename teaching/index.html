02 · The gap

Most AI tuition stops at the demo.

Building a working demo with an AI tool is now an afternoon's work. Understanding how AI works, and using the tools well, is covered by most courses. The harder and more valuable lessons start afterwards: when real people use what you have made, when something breaks, and when you are responsible for other people's data.

04 · Safety & governance

Governance, sized to whoever is building.

With businesses, I spend a good deal of time on AI governance: who can see the data, what a system is allowed to do, and who is accountable when something goes wrong. The same four questions apply whoever is building, a student's first app, a new tool at work, or a leadership team's first pilot, in words sized to fit. Most people are never asked them, and they are far easier to learn early than to unlearn later.

My data

What should never be typed into an AI tool, and why 'free' usually means you are paying some other way. Many AI tools also set a minimum age in their terms of use, worth checking before a minor uses one.

Other people's data

Collect the minimum, keep it safe and delete it when it is no longer needed. For anything likely to be used by children in the UK, the ICO's Children's Code is a useful checklist in its own right.

Who's accountable

A named owner for anything built, signed off by the right person before it goes live: a parent for a minor's project, a manager or compliance lead for a workplace one.

Checking the machine

AI tools can be confidently wrong, and can be steered by text hidden in what they read. So their work is checked, and nothing important is left to them unsupervised.

    05 · How it works

    Working with the people around the project.

    Sessions

    Mostly online. For a student, scheduled around the school timetable and term dates; for a team or a leader, around normal working patterns. Occasional in-person sessions are arranged where they help, for a launch, a demo day or a workshop, for instance.

    Keeping the right people informed

    A short written update at the end of each stage: what was learned, what was made, and what comes next. For a student that goes to parents; at work, to a manager or sponsor. Everyone involved is invited to the end-of-programme demo.

    Independence

    The aim is someone who can research, plan and solve problems without hand-holding. Support is stepped back as confidence grows, the same way autonomy is handed to AI systems in business.

    Checks

    Working with a minor, I am happy to undergo enhanced background checks and to agree ground rules for online sessions with the family at the outset. For workplace engagements, the usual contracting and confidentiality terms apply instead.
